    Bus and branch types from data sheets¶

    A BranchType and a BusType carry their impedances as formula strings in f, rho and l (formula syntax). This page shows how to turn a cable data sheet, an overhead-line conductor table and an earth-electrode geometry into those strings, with a worked example for the single-core cable NA2XS(F)2Y 1×300 RM/25 12/20 kV.

    Every number on this page is computed by types_from_datasheets.py, which evaluates each cable quantity three ways — the closed form, Pollaczek's integral and a groundinsight network — and the test suite checks the agreement stated below.

    Check the inputs before you use the types

    The data-sheet values are quoted with their source. Values that a data sheet does not give — the build-up between insulation and screen wires, the stranding factor of the conductor, the geometry of the overhead line — are estimates and are marked as such. Replace them by the values of your cable and line.

    What a branch type needs¶

    Field Quantity Symbol
    self_impedance_formula loop of the earthing conductor (cable screen, earth wire) with earth return Z_s
    mutual_impedance_formula coupling between the loop of the faulted phase conductor and that loop, both with earth return Z_m
    phase_impedance_formula loop of the faulted phase conductor with earth return, the screen bonded at both ends Z_red = Z_pp − Z_m²/Z_s
    grounding_conductor=True the branch has a screen or earth wire bonded to the buses –

    Each formula is the impedance of the whole branch: a per-km expression times l. The types below also set conductor_material and cross_section_mm2 of the screen, which the thermal check of the earthing conductors reads.

    Inputs¶

    Input Symbol Where it comes from
    DC resistance of the screen per km Rs′ data sheet or test certificate of the cable; else the EN 60228 value of the screen cross-section
    mean radius of the screen rs diameter over insulation plus the build-up to the middle of the screen wires
    DC resistance of the conductor per km Rc′ data sheet (EN 60228)
    geometric mean radius of the conductor GMRc conductor radius × e−1/4 for a solid round conductor
    outer diameter D data sheet; the axis distance of touching cables
    soil resistivity ρ measurement (EN 50522:2022, Annex L.1)
    frequencies f the study: 50 Hz and the harmonics of interest

    Screen–earth loop and coupling¶

    Carson's formula [3] with the equivalent earth-return depth \(D_E = 658\,\sqrt{\rho/f}\) m gives, per km,

    \[ Z_s' = R_s' + \pi^2\,10^{-4}\,f + j\,4\pi\,10^{-4}\,f\,\ln\frac{D_E}{r_s}, \qquad Z_m' = \pi^2\,10^{-4}\,f + j\,4\pi\,10^{-4}\,f\,\ln\frac{D_E}{r_s} \qquad [\Omega/\text{km}]. \]
    • \(\pi^2 10^{-4} f = \omega\mu_0/8\) is the resistance of the earth return, 0.0493 Ω/km at 50 Hz; \(4\pi 10^{-4} f = \omega\mu_0/(2\pi)\) is the reactance per unit of the logarithm, 0.0628 Ω/km at 50 Hz.
    • The core and its concentric screen link the same flux outside \(r_s\), so the mutual impedance has the same logarithm as the screen loop and \(Z_s' - Z_m' = R_s'\). The screen is treated as thin: its internal reactance is neglected and its transfer impedance is its DC resistance, which holds while the wire diameter is small against the skin depth (1.3 mm in copper at 2.5 kHz, against 0.75 mm wires here).
    • 658 is the rounded constant; the exact low-frequency limit of the earth-return integral is \(2e^{1/2-\gamma}/\sqrt{\omega\mu_0/\rho} = 658.87\sqrt{\rho/f}\) m (\(\gamma\) Euler's constant). The closed form assumes homogeneous soil, neglects displacement currents and holds while the conductor depth and radius are small against \(1/\lvert m\rvert\), \(m = \sqrt{j\omega\mu_0/\rho}\).

    For one line between ideally bonded stations the reduction factor is

    \[ r = \left\lvert 1 - \frac{Z_m}{Z_s} \right\rvert = \frac{R_s'}{\lvert Z_s' \rvert}, \]

    which is what ResultReductionFactor.value reproduces for a single branch. The reduction factor of EN 50522 in a network, value_current, depends on the electrodes as well; see Compare results with EN 50522 limits.

    Phase impedance for rings and meshes¶

    In a ring or mesh the source current divides over several routes, and phase_current_mode="auto" computes that split on a network of phase conductors. The impedance it needs is the loop of the faulted phase conductor with the screen bonded at both ends:

    \[ Z_{pp}' = R_c' + \pi^2\,10^{-4}\,f + j\,4\pi\,10^{-4}\,f\,\ln\frac{D_E}{\mathrm{GMR}_c}, \qquad Z_\text{red}' = Z_{pp}' - \frac{Z_m'^2}{Z_s'}. \]

    Eliminating the screen current of a branch from its two loop equations leaves exactly \(Z_\text{red}\) in front of the phase current. It is neither the positive-sequence impedance \(R_1 + jX_1\) nor the conductor resistance; for a branch without an earthing conductor it is \(Z_{pp}\). The pandapower importer can derive the formula from a line's r_ohm_per_km/x_ohm_per_km (ImportDefaults.derive_phase_impedance): that is the positive-sequence impedance, a proxy that splits a ring exactly only when all its routes have the same construction. In a radial network the split is fixed by the topology and the formula does not change the result; declare it anyway, so the type stays correct when the network gets a second infeed. How the split and the coupling are modelled is explained in Phase currents in rings and meshes.

    Worked example: NA2XS(F)2Y 1×300 RM/25 12/20 kV¶

    Data of the cable, ρ = 100 Ω·m, 50 Hz and the 5th harmonic (250 Hz):

    Quantity Value Source
    conductor Al, 300 mm², Rc′ = 0.100 Ω/km at 20 °C Prysmian data sheet [1], EN 60228 [2]
    screen Cu wires 25 mm², Rs′ = 0.727 Ω/km at 20 °C EN 60228 value for 25 mm² [2]
    insulation 5.5 mm, diameter over insulation 32.9 mm Prysmian data sheet [1], row 1x300RM/25
    outer diameter 41.9 mm Prysmian data sheet [1]
    semiconductive layers, swelling tape, wire diameter 0.6 mm, 0.4 mm, 0.75 mm estimates
    mean screen radius rs 32.9/2 + 0.6 + 0.4 + 0.75/2 = 17.825 mm from the rows above
    conductor radius, GMR 32.9/2 − 5.5 − 0.6 = 10.35 mm; GMR = 8.06 mm estimate, solid-conductor factor e−1/4
    laying depth h 0.7 m rating conditions of the data sheet [1]; only used for method (b)

    The screen radius enters only through a logarithm: ±5 % in rs moves r at 50 Hz by ±0.2 %. The screen resistance enters directly; at 20 °C it is the lower bound, and a warm screen gives a higher r (see the three-phase system).

    def earth_return_depth(rho, f):
    """Equivalent earth-return depth ``D_E = 658*sqrt(rho/f)`` in m."""
    return 658.0 * math.sqrt(rho / f)


def loop_impedance(r_dc, radius, rho, f):
    """
    Conductor-earth loop impedance per km after Carson, in Ohm/km.

    ``r_dc + pi²·1e-4·f + j·4·pi·1e-4·f·ln(D_E/radius)``. With ``r_dc = 0``
    and the screen radius it is the mutual impedance of a conductor and its
    concentric screen, both with earth return.
    """
    return complex(
        r_dc + math.pi**2 * 1e-4 * f,
        4 * math.pi * 1e-4 * f * math.log(earth_return_depth(rho, f) / radius),
    )


def loop_expression(r_dc, radius, parallel=1):
    """
    The same loop impedance as a formula expression in ``f`` and ``rho``.

    ``parallel`` conductors of resistance ``r_dc`` each are written as
    ``r_dc/parallel``; ``radius`` is then their geometric mean radius.
    """
    if not r_dc:
        resistance = ""
    elif parallel == 1:
        resistance = f"{r_dc!r} + "
    else:
        resistance = f"{r_dc!r}/{parallel} + "
    return (
        f"({resistance}pi**2*1e-4*f + j*4*pi*1e-4*f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/{radius!r}))"
    )


def reduced_phase_expression(z_pp, z_m, z_s):
    """``Z_red = Z_pp - Z_m²/Z_s`` as a formula expression."""
    return f"({z_pp} - {z_m}**2/{z_s})"

    z_s_expr = loop_expression(R_SCREEN, R_SCREEN_MEAN)
z_m_expr = loop_expression(0.0, R_SCREEN_MEAN)
z_pp_expr = loop_expression(R_CORE, GMR_CONDUCTOR)

cable = gi.BranchType(
    name="NA2XS(F)2Y 1x300RM/25 12/20 kV, own screen",
    grounding_conductor=True,
    self_impedance_formula=f"{z_s_expr}*l",
    mutual_impedance_formula=f"{z_m_expr}*l",
    phase_impedance_formula=f"{reduced_phase_expression(z_pp_expr, z_m_expr, z_s_expr)}*l",
    conductor_material="Cu",
    cross_section_mm2=25.0,
)

    Three ways to the same numbers¶

    (a) Closed form — the formulas above, evaluated in plain Python.

    (b) Pollaczek's integral [5] for a conductor buried at depth \(h\) in homogeneous soil, evaluated with scipy.integrate.quad:

    \[ Z_E' = \frac{j\omega\mu_0}{2\pi}\left[K_0(m r) - K_0(2mh) + 2\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-2h\sqrt{\lambda^2+m^2}}}{\lambda + \sqrt{\lambda^2+m^2}}\,d\lambda\right], \qquad Z_m' = Z_E'(r_s),\quad Z_s' = R_s' + Z_E'(r_s). \]
    MU0 = 4e-7 * math.pi  # H/m


def pollaczek_earth_impedance(radius, depth, rho, f):
    """
    Earth-return part of a conductor-earth loop, conductor buried at ``depth``.

    Pollaczek's integral for homogeneous soil, displacement currents
    neglected, in Ohm/km:
    ``jωμ0/(2π)·[K0(m·r) − K0(m·2h) + 2∫ exp(−2h·√(λ²+m²))/(λ+√(λ²+m²)) dλ]``
    with ``m = √(jωμ0/ρ)``.
    """
    omega = 2 * math.pi * f
    m = cmath.sqrt(1j * omega * MU0 / rho)

    def integrand(lam):
        s = cmath.sqrt(lam * lam + m * m)
        return cmath.exp(-2 * depth * s) / (lam + s)

    upper = 40.0 / depth  # the integrand is below exp(-80) beyond
    points = sorted({abs(m), 10 * abs(m), 1 / (2 * depth)})
    parts = [
        integrate.quad(
            lambda lam, part=part: getattr(integrand(lam), part),
            0.0, upper, points=points, limit=400, epsabs=0.0, epsrel=1e-11,
        )[0]
        for part in ("real", "imag")
    ]
    bracket = (
        special.kv(0, m * radius) - special.kv(0, m * 2 * depth) + 2 * complex(*parts)
    )
    return 1j * omega * MU0 / (2 * math.pi) * bracket * 1e3


def internal_reactance(f):
    """Internal reactance of a solid round conductor, ``ωμ0/(8π)``, Ohm/km."""
    return 1j * 2 * math.pi * f * MU0 / (8 * math.pi) * 1e3

    • (a) against (b) shows how good the \(D_E\) approximation is. At 50 Hz the earth-return resistance differs by 0.17 %, the reactance by about 1e-6, and r by 4.2·10⁻⁵ (1.4·10⁻⁵ at 250 Hz). At 50 Hz most of that is the rounded constant: with 658.87 instead of 658 the difference in r drops to 4.8·10⁻⁶. The rest is the burial depth; it shrinks in proportion to \(h\).
    • Over 16.7 Hz … 2.5 kHz and 10 … 10 000 Ω·m (depth 0.7 m) the closed form stays within 1.5·10⁻⁴ of the integral in r and within 0.5 % in \(\lvert Z_s'\rvert\) and \(\lvert Z_m'\rvert\); the largest deviation in r on a 40 × 40 grid of that range is 1.3·10⁻⁴, near 400 Hz and 10 Ω·m. The integral itself is checked against an independent quadrature (mpmath) to 1e-9 and against its analytic low-frequency limit.

    Three-phase system: the other two screens¶

    A three-phase cable system of single-core cables has three screens. With all of them bonded at both ends, the faulted core drives current into all three, and the three screens return it in parallel. groundinsight has no coupling between the screens of one branch, so the system is one branch whose type describes the screen bundle: the screens in parallel, seen from the faulted core. Eliminating the individual screen currents gives

    \[ Z_{s,\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{\mathbf 1^T Z_{ss}^{-1}\mathbf 1},\qquad Z_{m,\text{eq}} = Z_{s,\text{eq}}\;\mathbf 1^T Z_{ss}^{-1} z_c , \]

    with \(Z_{ss}\) the 3×3 matrix of screen loop and screen–screen impedances and \(z_c\) the couplings of the faulted core to each screen. In trefoil every row of \(Z_{ss}\) has the same sum, and the result is exactly the bundle formula: resistance \(R_s'/3\), and \(\mathrm{GMR} = (r_s s^2)^{1/3}\) in both logarithms (\(s\) = axis distance = outer diameter for touching cables), so again \(Z_{s,\text{eq}} - Z_{m,\text{eq}} = R_s'/3\).

    def bundle_equivalent(positions, faulted, r_screen, r_dc, rho, f):
    """
    Equivalent screen-bundle impedances seen by one faulted core, Ohm/km.

    ``positions`` are the cable axes (x, y) in m; the screens are bonded at
    both ends, so they share one voltage drop. Eliminating their individual
    currents from ``Z_ss·I = U·1 − z_c·I_core`` gives
    ``Z_s,eq = 1/(1ᵀ Z_ss⁻¹ 1)`` and ``Z_m,eq = Z_s,eq · 1ᵀ Z_ss⁻¹ z_c``.
    """
    n = len(positions)
    z_ss = np.empty((n, n), dtype=complex)
    z_c = np.empty(n, dtype=complex)
    for i in range(n):
        for k in range(n):
            d = r_screen if i == k else math.dist(positions[i], positions[k])
            z_ss[i, k] = loop_impedance(r_dc if i == k else 0.0, d, rho, f)
        d = r_screen if i == faulted else math.dist(positions[i], positions[faulted])
        z_c[i] = loop_impedance(0.0, d, rho, f)
    ones = np.ones(n)
    z_s_eq = 1 / (ones @ np.linalg.solve(z_ss, ones))
    z_m_eq = z_s_eq * (ones @ np.linalg.solve(z_ss, z_c))
    return complex(z_s_eq), complex(z_m_eq)


def screen_current_shares(positions, faulted, r_screen, r_dc, rho, f):
    """
    Share of each screen in the bundle current, screens ideally bonded.

    With no voltage drop along the screens the coupling alone drives them:
    ``I = −Z_ss⁻¹ z_c`` per ampere in the faulted core. Returns
    ``|I_k| / |Σ I|`` for every screen.
    """
    n = len(positions)
    z_ss = np.empty((n, n), dtype=complex)
    z_c = np.empty(n, dtype=complex)
    for i in range(n):
        for k in range(n):
            d = r_screen if i == k else math.dist(positions[i], positions[k])
            z_ss[i, k] = loop_impedance(r_dc if i == k else 0.0, d, rho, f)
        d = r_screen if i == faulted else math.dist(positions[i], positions[faulted])
        z_c[i] = loop_impedance(0.0, d, rho, f)
    currents = -np.linalg.solve(z_ss, z_c)
    return np.abs(currents) / abs(currents.sum())

    50 Hz 0.703286 0.341570
    250 Hz 0.219869 0.080223

    The other two screens halve the reduction factor at 50 Hz. Which type is right depends on how the screens are bonded: all three at both ends — the usual case — is the bundle; a cable system whose screens are bonded at one end only, or cross-bonded, needs its own derivation.

    • Flat formation. The rows of \(Z_{ss}\) differ, and the bundle formula with geometric mean distances is an approximation: for touching cables it is off by 0.1 … 0.2 % in r at 50 Hz and by 2 … 5 % at 250 Hz (outer and middle phase faulted). Compute the bundle with the matrix reduction (bundle_equivalent in the snippet) at the study frequencies and check the formula against it.
    • Thermal check of the screens. check_conductor_limits compares the current of the branch — here the bundle — with cross_section_mm2. The three screens do not share it equally: with the screens ideally bonded, the coupling drives 35.9 % of the bundle current through the screen of the faulted core at 50 Hz, and 48.1 % at 250 Hz (a voltage drop along the bundle adds an equal part to every screen). The type therefore carries 25 mm²/0.359 = 69.7 mm² instead of 75 mm², which covers the faulted core's screen at 50 Hz; with a large harmonic content use the larger share.
    • Screen temperature. r grows with the screen resistance. With the screens at 70 °C instead of 20 °C (factor 304.5/254.5 for copper) r of the trefoil system at 50 Hz rises from 0.342 to 0.397. The 20 °C value is the lower bound and gives the lower earth potential rise.

    Overhead line with earth wire¶

    For a line whose earth wire is bonded to the towers and the stations, the earth wire takes the place of the screen. With the earth wire's resistance \(R_{ew}'\) and geometric mean radius, and the distance \(d\) between the faulted phase conductor and the earth wire:

    \[ Z_s' = R_{ew}' + \pi^2 10^{-4} f + j\,4\pi 10^{-4} f \ln\frac{D_E}{\mathrm{GMR}_{ew}}, \qquad Z_m' = \pi^2 10^{-4} f + j\,4\pi 10^{-4} f \ln\frac{D_E}{d}. \]

    When the faulted phase is not known, or the line is transposed, use for \(d\) the geometric mean of the distances of the three phases to the earth wire; two earth wires form a bundle like the screens above. Model each span as a branch and each tower as a bus whose electrode is the tower footing.

    Example: phase conductor 94-AL1/15-ST1A (Al/St 95/15, 0.3060 Ω/km, Ø 13.60 mm), earth wire 47-AL1/8-ST1A (0.6129 Ω/km, Ø 9.45 mm) [4], earth wire 2 m above the faulted phase at 10 m.

    # 20 kV overhead line: phase conductor 94-AL1/15-ST1A (Al/St 95/15), earth wire
# 47-AL1/8-ST1A (Al/St 50/8), EN 50182 values; geometry: estimate.
R_EARTH_WIRE = 0.6129  # Ohm/km at 20 °C
GMR_EARTH_WIRE = round(math.exp(-0.25) * 9.45e-3 / 2, 8)  # m, solid-conductor approximation
R_PHASE_OHL = 0.3060  # Ohm/km at 20 °C
GMR_PHASE_OHL = round(math.exp(-0.25) * 13.6e-3 / 2, 8)  # m, solid-conductor approximation
H_PHASE, H_EARTH_WIRE = 10.0, 12.0  # m above ground, earth wire above the faulted phase
D_PHASE_EARTH_WIRE = H_EARTH_WIRE - H_PHASE  # m

z_ew_expr = loop_expression(R_EARTH_WIRE, GMR_EARTH_WIRE)
z_pe_expr = loop_expression(0.0, D_PHASE_EARTH_WIRE)
overhead_line = gi.BranchType(
    name="20 kV OHL 94-AL1/15-ST1A with earth wire 47-AL1/8-ST1A",
    grounding_conductor=True,
    self_impedance_formula=f"{z_ew_expr}*l",
    mutual_impedance_formula=f"{z_pe_expr}*l",
    phase_impedance_formula=(
        f"{reduced_phase_expression(loop_expression(R_PHASE_OHL, GMR_PHASE_OHL), z_pe_expr, z_ew_expr)}*l"
    ),
)


def carson_earth_impedance(h_i, h_j, x, d, rho, f):
    """
    Carson's loop or mutual impedance of overhead conductors, in Ohm/km.

    With earth return, without the internal impedance:
    ``jωμ0/(2π)·ln(D/d) + jωμ0/π·∫ exp(−(h_i+h_j)λ)·cos(xλ)/(λ+√(λ²+m²)) dλ``,
    ``D`` the distance to the image conductor.
    """
    omega = 2 * math.pi * f
    m = cmath.sqrt(1j * omega * MU0 / rho)
    height = h_i + h_j

    def integrand(lam):
        return cmath.exp(-height * lam) * math.cos(x * lam) / (lam + cmath.sqrt(lam * lam + m * m))

    upper = 80.0 / height
    points = sorted({abs(m), 10 * abs(m), 1 / height})
    parts = [
        integrate.quad(
            lambda lam, part=part: getattr(integrand(lam), part),
            0.0, upper, points=points, limit=400, epsabs=0.0, epsrel=1e-11,
        )[0]
        for part in ("real", "imag")
    ]
    image = math.hypot(x, height)
    z = 1j * omega * MU0 / (2 * math.pi) * math.log(image / d)
    return (z + 1j * omega * MU0 / math.pi * complex(*parts)) * 1e3

    overhead_line = gi.BranchType(
    name="20 kV OHL 94-AL1/15-ST1A with earth wire 47-AL1/8-ST1A",
    grounding_conductor=True,
    self_impedance_formula=(
        "(0.6129 + pi**2*1e-4*f + j*4*pi*1e-4*f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/0.00367983))*l"
    ),
    mutual_impedance_formula=(
        "(pi**2*1e-4*f + j*4*pi*1e-4*f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/2.0))*l"
    ),
    phase_impedance_formula=(
        "((0.306 + pi**2*1e-4*f + j*4*pi*1e-4*f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/0.00529585))"
        " - (pi**2*1e-4*f + j*4*pi*1e-4*f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/2.0))**2"
        "/(0.6129 + pi**2*1e-4*f + j*4*pi*1e-4*f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/0.00367983)))*l"
    ),
)
    f r (a) closed form r (b) Carson's integral r (c) groundinsight
    50 Hz 0.712107 0.711893 0.712107
    250 Hz 0.551584 0.550026 0.551584

    Method (b) is Carson's integral for conductors above ground. Overhead conductors sit much higher than a buried cable, so the closed form loses accuracy sooner: 2·10⁻⁴ in r at 50 Hz, 1.6·10⁻³ at 250 Hz, and up to 2·10⁻² at 2.5 kHz in 10 Ω·m soil.

    Electrode impedance of a station¶

    A BusType needs the impedance of the station's electrode to remote earth, Z_B — not the earthing impedance of the network at that station (see the glossary). Two sources:

    • A measurement. An earthing-impedance measurement at a station whose cable screens are connected measures the network's driving-point impedance, not Z_B. Measure with the screens and earth wires disconnected, or derive Z_B from the measured value and the network. groundmeas manages and evaluates such measurements, including impedance against frequency and soil resistivity.
    • A standard formula for the electrode geometry. The common ones and two published variants each, at ρ = 100 Ω·m:

    ring around 10 m × 10 m, D = 1.13·√A 2ρ/(3D) (rough rule [7], [8]) 5.90 Ω ρ/(2π²D)·(ln(8D/d) + ln(4D/s)), d = 10 mm, s = 2·0.5 m (Dwight [6]) 5.79 Ω

    The two variants agree within 4 %; the soil model behind them — homogeneous ρ — is the larger uncertainty. Frequency dependence is a modelling choice. The formulas give a resistance, the same at every frequency. A measured impedance with an inductive part is written so that the reactance scales with frequency (0.42 + j*0.08*f/50 above); that form also tends to the resistance at 0 Hz by itself.

    At 0 Hz¶

    DC studies and every FFT transient study evaluate the formulas at 0 Hz, where f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/r) is 0·∞. The evaluator approaches 0 Hz numerically (formula syntax); for the types on this page it returns:

    Formula Value at 0 Hz Message
    self_impedance_formula Rs′·l, the DC resistance of the screen none
    phase_impedance_formula Rc′·l, the DC resistance of the conductor none
    mutual_impedance_formula about π²·10⁻¹²·l Ω — zero for every practical purpose, r = 1 none

    The mutual impedance tends to zero: its real part \(\pi^2 10^{-4} f\) has no constant term, so what the approach ends on, the value at 10⁻⁸ Hz, is all that is left of it. A test relative to the value cannot judge such a limit — the real part changes by 90 % of itself per decade however small it is — but the step shrinks ten-fold per decade, as for anything linear in f, and the evaluator accepts that as converged at every length. A formula that behaves like \(\sqrt{f}\) or \(\log f\) near 0 Hz still gets a DCLimitWarning "converges only slowly". The electrode formulas contain no f and are unchanged at 0 Hz.

    Check a new type¶

    • Run a one-branch network and compare value with \(\lvert 1 - Z_m/Z_s\rvert\) of the closed form, as method (c) does.
    • Compare r at 50 Hz with the typical values that EN 50522:2022 lists for cables and lines (Annex I).
    • Evaluate the formulas at the lowest ρ, the lowest frequency and the shortest length of the model: a negative real part or a NaN is refused (formula syntax), and so is a real part that runs to −∞ at 0 Hz, which a log(f) term without a factor f produces.
