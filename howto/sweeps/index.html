    Parameter sweeps and statistics¶

    A single solve answers one question; a study asks it over a range — the soil resistivity, the characteristic of an electrode, the harmonic content, the fault location. This guide runs one fault over such a grid with gi.run_sweep, stacks the results into one frame and reduces it with gi.summarize and gi.classify. The script is sweeps.py; its output is at the end of the page.

    The example is the 20 kV feeder of the EN 50522 how-to: substation UW and five stations bonded through the screens of a cable system, earth fault at S3, 1000 A at 50 Hz and 50 A at 250 Hz.

    cable_system = gi.BranchType(
    name="3x NA2XS(F)2Y 1x300RM/25 12/20 kV, trefoil",
    grounding_conductor=True,
    self_impedance_formula=(
        "(0.727/3 + pi**2*1e-4*f + j*4*pi*1e-4*f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/0.03151272))*l"
    ),
    mutual_impedance_formula=(
        "(pi**2*1e-4*f + j*4*pi*1e-4*f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/0.03151272))*l"
    ),
)
substation = gi.BusType(name="substation", system_type="Substation", voltage_level=20.0,
                        impedance_formula="0.5")
station = gi.BusType(name="station", system_type="Station", voltage_level=20.0,
                     impedance_formula="2*rho/(3*1.13*sqrt(100.0))")  # ring electrode

STATIONS = ["S1", "S2", "S3", "S4", "S5"]
FAULT_BUS = "S3"
net = gi.create_network(name="feeder", frequencies=[50.0, 250.0])
gi.create_bus(name="UW", type=substation, specific_earth_resistance=100.0, network=net)
for name in STATIONS:
    gi.create_bus(name=name, type=station, specific_earth_resistance=100.0, network=net)
names = ["UW"] + STATIONS
for i, (a, b) in enumerate(zip(names[:-1], names[1:], strict=True), start=1):
    gi.create_branch(name=f"K{i}", type=cable_system, from_bus=a, to_bus=b, length=1.2,
                     specific_earth_resistance=100.0, network=net)
gi.create_source(name="infeed", bus="UW", values={50.0: 1000.0, 250.0: 50.0}, network=net)
gi.create_fault(name="F", bus=FAULT_BUS, scalings={50.0: 1.0, 250.0: 1.0}, network=net)
before = net.model_dump_json()

    One point per parameter combination¶

    A gi.SweepPoint is a label plus the changes that define it:

    Field Changes Use it for
    bus_rho specific_earth_resistance of the named buses, with their bus-type formula evaluated again the soil around the electrodes
    bus_impedance the impedance table Bus.impedance of the named buses, written directly — the formula is bypassed a measured or fitted characteristic
    fault the fault solved at this point the fault location
    fault_scalings the scalings of the fault for this point the harmonic content
    parameters nothing — copied into every result row as columns plotting against the physical parameter

    bus_rho changes the buses only: the specific_earth_resistance of the branches, which enters the earth-return terms of a Carson-type cable formula, stays as it is. To vary the soil along the cables as well, give the points a fault and network of their own, or build one network per soil value.

    RHO_VALUES = [30.0, 100.0, 300.0, 1000.0]  # Ohm*m at the station electrodes
soil_points = [
    gi.SweepPoint(label=f"rho={rho:g}", bus_rho={name: rho for name in STATIONS},
                  parameters={"rho_Ohm_m": rho})
    for rho in RHO_VALUES
]
soil = gi.run_sweep(net, fault="F", points=soil_points)

    gi.run_sweep(network, fault=..., points=...) solves the fault once per point, undoes the point's changes after the solve, and when it ends — finished, failed or cancelled — the network is left as it was: same stored results, same active fault, same paths (the script checks it with model_dump_json()). on_error decides what a failing point does: with "record" (the default) it is noted in SweepResult.failures with its message and the sweep goes on; with "raise" the first failure ends it. progress= and should_cancel= report and stop a long sweep.

    A catalogue of rho-f characteristics¶

    gi.rho_f_points turns a catalogue of five-parameter rho-f vectors — the form \(Z = k_1\rho + (k_2 + jk_3)f + (k_4 + jk_5)\rho f\) that groundfield exports — into points that set the impedance table of one bus. It refuses a vector whose real part is not positive at some frequency, naming the label and the frequency, because the solver would reject it anyway.

    K_VECTORS = {  # Z = k1*rho + (k2 + j*k3)*f + (k4 + j*k5)*rho*f, illustrative fits
    "small electrode": (0.10, 0.0, 2e-4, 0.0, 1e-6),
    "ring electrode": (0.059, 0.0, 2e-4, 0.0, 1e-6),
    "meshed grid": (0.02, 0.0, 2e-4, 0.0, 1e-6),
}
characteristic = gi.run_sweep(net, fault="F", points=gi.rho_f_points(
    bus=FAULT_BUS, k_vectors=K_VECTORS, frequencies=net.frequencies, rho=100.0))

    Reading the results¶

    SweepResult.buses(), .branches() (only with collect_branches=True), .impedances() and .cuts() (only with cuts=) stack the frames of the points: the columns of res_buses, res_branches, res_all_impedances or CutAnalysis.to_polars, preceded by point (the label) and one column per key of parameters; rho_f_points adds bus, rho_Ohm_m and k1 … k5. The RMS rows have is_rms true, as in a single solve. Columns: Result tables.

    Summarise and classify¶

    summarize — gi.summarize(frame, value, by=...) — reduces one column to n, n_null, n_nan, mean, std, min, quantiles (p05, p50, p95 by default) and max, per group of by=. Read n_nan first: a failed point recorded as NaN propagates into the mean and the quantiles. classify — gi.classify(frame, value, edges, labels=...) — adds a column with the band of each row; a null or NaN value gets no band rather than the highest one.

    Neither function knows a limit. The permissible values of EN 50522 depend on the fault duration, the edition and the measures taken, so the edges come from your study — Compare results with EN 50522 limits shows where they come from. The bands below are illustrative.

    stacked = pl.concat([soil.buses(), characteristic.buses()], how="diagonal")
fault_bus_rms = stacked.filter((pl.col("bus_name") == FAULT_BUS) & pl.col("is_rms"))
summary = gi.summarize(fault_bus_rms, "EPR_V")
EDGES_V = [300.0, 600.0]  # bands of your study -- not limits of a standard
LABELS = ["up to 300 V", "300 V to 600 V", "above 600 V"]
classes = gi.classify(fault_bus_rms, "EPR_V", EDGES_V, labels=LABELS)

         rho=100        384.6  300 V to 600 V
         rho=300        622.8  above 600 V
        rho=1000        788.8  above 600 V

    U_E RMS is the total RMS of the earth potential rise of S3 over 50 Hz and 250 Hz. The point rho=100 reproduces the worked example of the EN 50522 how-to (384.6 V); ring electrode describes the same electrode by its rho-f fit and differs only by the small reactance the fit carries. The test suite recomputes every point with the independent reference solver of the tests, with the station impedances evaluated from the formulas.
