    Compare results with EN 50522 limits¶

    EN 50522 requires that the touch voltages of an installation stay within permissible values that depend on the fault duration. This page shows which groundinsight result feeds which quantity of that verification, how the procedure runs, and which parts of it the model does not cover. It refers to the clauses of EN 50522:2022 by number and name and does not reproduce the permissible values: take them from the edition you apply.

    Scope of the results

    groundinsight is a research and engineering tool. Its results support, but do not replace, an assessment according to EN 50522 / IEC 61936-1 by a qualified person, including the measurements the standards require. The software is provided under the MIT License, without warranty of any kind.

    What the model gives you¶

    Per fault and per frequency, groundinsight computes the fault current, the earth potential rise of every bus, the shield currents, the earth current and the earthing impedance at the fault location, and the reduction factor of EN 50522. It does not compute:

    • touch voltages, step voltages and surface potentials — there is no model of the soil surface;
    • additional resistances such as footwear or a surface layer, which the prospective touch voltage of the standard takes into account (Annex A);
    • the permissible touch voltage itself;
    • potentials transferred to other systems (Clause 6);
    • the dimensioning for corrosion and mechanical strength (5.2); the thermal check of earthing conductors is available separately (check_conductor_limits, check_node_limits, see Analysis).

    Which result is which symbol¶

    EN 50522 Meaning groundinsight In the tables
    3I_0 earth-fault current at the fault location ResultReductionFactor.i_fault — the source currents that reach the fault, times the fault scalings I_F_A of res_fault()
    r reduction factor ResultReductionFactor.value_current r_current of res_fault(); reduction_factor_current of res_all_impedances()
    I_E earth current, r·3I_0 ResultReductionFactor.i_earth I_E_A of res_fault()
    Z_E earthing impedance ResultReductionFactor.z_earthing, equal to ResultGroundingImpedance.value Z_E_Ohm, Z_E_degree of res_fault(); grounding_impedance_Ohm of res_all_impedances()
    U_E earth potential rise ResultReductionFactor.u_earthing, equal to the uepr_freq of the fault bus U_E_V of res_fault(); EPR_V of the fault bus in res_buses()
    U_T, U_vT touch voltage, prospective touch voltage not computed –
    U_Tp permissible touch voltage not part of the package EN 50522:2022, 5.4.1 and Annexes A, B
    t_F fault duration not used by the solve from the protection
    • value, the column reduction_factor, is not the r of EN 50522. It is the EPR ratio with and without the inductive coupling — a property of the line, blind to the electrodes (conventions).
    • Z_E is the driving-point impedance of the whole earthing network at the fault bus, the stations bonded through screens and earth wires included — what an earthing-impedance measurement at the station sees. It is not the electrode of the station, Z_B (glossary).
    • Complex values are in the package's sign frame (conventions); the verification uses magnitudes.
    • Fault.t_k_s is the short-circuit duration T_k of IEC 60909-0 for the thermal checks. The fault duration t_F of the touch-voltage verification comes from the same protection, but the solve does not use it.

    The procedure¶

    1. Fault current and fault duration. Get 3I_0 at the fault location — from the network operator, or from a short-circuit calculation after IEC 60909-0 (a pandapower case can be imported, see I/O) — per frequency as RMS values, and the fault duration t_F from the protection settings.
    2. Model. Build the network with branch types from data sheets (how-to) and the electrode impedances Z_B of the stations. Put the fault current at the infeed as Source.values, the fault at the station under study.
    3. Solve and read U_E, Z_E, I_E and r per frequency, and U_E as the total RMS over the frequencies (see Harmonics and DC): net.res_fault(fault) reports all of them in one frame (below).
    4. Permissible touch voltage. Read U_Tp(t_F) from EN 50522:2022, 5.4.1; Annexes A and B derive it and the prospective permissible touch voltage.
    5. Design procedure. Apply the procedure of 5.4.3 (the flow chart of the earthing-system design, Figure 9): it compares U_E with U_Tp, taking the recognised measures M of Annex E into account, and tells you when the touch voltages themselves have to be determined — by measurement (Annex H), or by a calculation of surface potentials, which groundinsight does not do. An installation that is part of a global earthing system follows its own branch of the procedure (Annex N).
    6. Measure. Clause 8 and Annex L describe the measurements of the earthing impedance (L.2), the earth potential rise (L.3) and touch voltages (L.4). Compare them with the model; the model does not replace them.
    7. Document the assessment (Clause 10).

    Worked example¶

    A 20 kV feeder: substation UW (0.5 Ω) and five stations with ring electrodes, bonded through the screens of the three-phase cable system of the data-sheet how-to in sections of 1.2 km, ρ = 100 Ω·m. Fault at station S3, with 1000 A at 50 Hz and 50 A at 250 Hz from the infeed (illustrative values).

    cable_system = gi.BranchType(
    name="3x NA2XS(F)2Y 1x300RM/25 12/20 kV, trefoil, screens bonded both ends",
    grounding_conductor=True,
    self_impedance_formula=(
        "(0.727/3 + pi**2*1e-4*f + j*4*pi*1e-4*f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/0.03151272))*l"
    ),
    mutual_impedance_formula=(
        "(pi**2*1e-4*f + j*4*pi*1e-4*f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/0.03151272))*l"
    ),
    phase_impedance_formula=(
        "((0.1 + pi**2*1e-4*f + j*4*pi*1e-4*f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/0.00806059))"
        " - (pi**2*1e-4*f + j*4*pi*1e-4*f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/0.03151272))**2"
        "/(0.727/3 + pi**2*1e-4*f + j*4*pi*1e-4*f*log(658*sqrt(rho/f)/0.03151272)))*l"
    ),
)
substation = gi.BusType(
    name="substation", system_type="Substation", voltage_level=20.0,
    impedance_formula="0.5",
)
station = gi.BusType(  # ring earth electrode around 10 m x 10 m
    name="station", system_type="Station", voltage_level=20.0,
    impedance_formula="2*rho/(3*1.13*sqrt(100.0))",
)

FREQUENCIES = [50.0, 250.0]
SOURCE_VALUES = {50.0: 1000.0, 250.0: 50.0}  # A RMS, 3I_0 per frequency
FAULT_BUS = "S3"

net = gi.create_network(name="feeder", frequencies=FREQUENCIES)
names = ["UW", "S1", "S2", "S3", "S4", "S5"]
gi.create_bus(name="UW", type=substation, specific_earth_resistance=100.0, network=net)
for name in names[1:]:
    gi.create_bus(name=name, type=station, specific_earth_resistance=100.0, network=net)
for i, (a, b) in enumerate(zip(names[:-1], names[1:], strict=True), start=1):
    gi.create_branch(
        name=f"K{i}", type=cable_system, from_bus=a, to_bus=b, length=1.2,
        specific_earth_resistance=100.0, network=net,
    )
gi.create_source(name="infeed", bus="UW", values=SOURCE_VALUES, network=net)
gi.create_fault(name="F", bus=FAULT_BUS, scalings={50.0: 1.0, 250.0: 1.0}, network=net)

    result = gi.run_fault(net, "F")  # the fault by name (positional)
rf = result.reduction_factor

ROWS = []
for f in FREQUENCIES:
    i_f = abs(complex(rf.i_fault[f].real, rf.i_fault[f].imag))  # 3I_0
    r = rf.value_current[f]  # EN 50522 reduction factor
    i_e = abs(complex(rf.i_earth[f].real, rf.i_earth[f].imag))  # I_E = r * 3I_0
    z_e = abs(complex(rf.z_earthing[f].real, rf.z_earthing[f].imag))  # Z_E
    u_e = abs(complex(rf.u_earthing[f].real, rf.u_earthing[f].imag))  # U_E
    ROWS.append({"f": f, "I_F": i_f, "r": r, "I_E": i_e, "Z_E": z_e, "U_E": u_e,
                 "chain": r * i_f * z_e})

U_E_RMS = math.sqrt(sum(row["U_E"] ** 2 for row in ROWS))  # total RMS over frequencies

    U_TP_V = None  # U_Tp(t_F) from EN 50522:2022, 5.4.1 / Annex B, for your fault duration

if U_TP_V is None:
    verdict = "U_Tp not set: read it from EN 50522:2022 for the fault duration t_F"
else:
    verdict = "U_E <= U_Tp" if U_E_RMS <= U_TP_V else "U_E > U_Tp: follow the design procedure"

    f 3I0 r IE |ZE| |UE| r·3I0·|ZE|
    50 Hz 1000.0 A 0.2165 216.5 A 1.189 Ω 384.4 V 257.3 V
    250 Hz 50.0 A 0.0766 3.8 A 2.232 Ω 9.10 V 8.56 V

    U_E as total RMS: 384.6 V — the value to compare with U_Tp(t_F).

    |U_E| is the earth potential rise of S3 in res_buses(), and |Z_E| is the driving-point impedance an independent nodal solve of the same electrodes and screens gives; the test suite checks both.

    The key figures in one frame¶

    The script reads the quantities from the result objects to show where each one lives. net.res_fault(fault) puts them side by side in one frame — one row per frequency and an RMS row, read from the stored result without solving again: I_F_A is 3I_0, r_current is r, I_E_A is I_E, Z_E_Ohm (with Z_E_degree) is Z_E and U_E_V is U_E. The column r_epr of the same frame is the EPR-based factor, not the r of EN 50522 (Result tables).

    KEY_FIGURES = net.res_fault("F").select(  # one row per frequency and an RMS row
    "frequency_Hz", "is_rms", "I_F_A", "r_current", "I_E_A", "Z_E_Ohm", "U_E_V"
)
    Output of en50522_check.py, last lines
    res_fault    I_F_A r_current   I_E_A  Z_E_Ohm    U_E_V
    50 Hz   1000.0    0.2165   216.5    1.189   384.45
   250 Hz     50.0    0.0766     3.8    2.232     9.10
      RMS   1001.2         -   216.5        -   384.56

    On the RMS row the ratio and the impedance are empty (null); U_E_V there is the total RMS of U_E over the frequencies, the value to compare with U_Tp(t_F). The test suite recomputes every figure of the frame with an independent nodal solver.

    The chain \(U_E = r \cdot 3I_0 \cdot Z_E\) of the standard is a hand formula. It holds where the standard's lumped picture holds — a long, homogeneous cable run with the fault far from the infeed — and there the model reproduces it. On this short feeder, with the fault two stations from either end, it gives 257 V at 50 Hz instead of the 384 V the model computes, 33 % low. Compare U_E itself with the permissible value; the difference to the chain shows how far the installation is from the lumped picture.

    Harmonics and DC¶

    The permissible touch voltages of EN 50522 are given for the power frequency; the standard applies to installations with nominal frequencies up to 60 Hz. For a fault current with harmonics or a DC component, compare the total RMS of U_E over all frequencies — 384.6 V above — with the curve U_Tp(t_F). For the body current this is a conservative choice: IEC 60479-2:2019 describes the effect of a current at a higher frequency by a frequency factor, the ratio of the threshold current at that frequency to the threshold at 50/60 Hz, and the thresholds of perception, let-go and ventricular fibrillation rise with frequency. Counting every component with its full RMS value against the 50/60 Hz curve therefore overstates the effect of the harmonics on the body current. The impedance of the skin, however, falls with frequency (DIN IEC/TS 60479-1 (VDE V 0140-479-1):2007-05, 4.2), which works the other way; whether the total RMS is conservative in terms of the touch voltage as well is not shown here.

    A DC component enters the total RMS with its full magnitude. IEC 60479-1:2018 treats the effects of direct current in a clause of its own; for the fault durations of interest its thresholds lie above the 50/60 Hz thresholds, so counting a DC component against the 50/60 Hz curve overstates its effect on the body current as well.

    EPR is an upper bound of the touch voltage¶

    The touch voltage is the part of U_E that a person bridges between a touched part and the point where the feet stand. For a person standing in the potential funnel of the same earthing system, the surface potential there lies between remote earth and U_E, so the touch voltage cannot exceed U_E. A result U_E ≤ U_Tp therefore covers the touch voltage; U_E > U_Tp does not mean that the touch voltage is too high, only that it has to be determined. Potentials transferred from other installations (Clause 6) are outside this bound.
