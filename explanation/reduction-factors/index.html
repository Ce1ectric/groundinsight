    Reduction factors and earthing impedance¶

    groundinsight reports two reduction factors for every fault and frequency, together with the quantities of the EN 50522 chain \(U_E = r \cdot 3I_0 \cdot Z_E\) at the fault bus. This page defines each of them, shows when they agree and when they do not, and says which one the standard means. Where each number appears in the results is in the glossary; how they feed the verification of the standard is in Compare results with EN 50522 limits.

    Field of ResultReductionFactor Column of res_all_impedances() Meaning
    value reduction_factor EPR at the fault bus with coupling over EPR without coupling
    value_current reduction_factor_current \(\lvert I_E\rvert / \lvert 3I_0\rvert\), the reduction factor r of EN 50522
    u_earthing — \(U_E\), the EPR of the fault bus
    z_earthing grounding_impedance_Ohm, grounding_impedance_deg \(Z_E\), the driving-point impedance of the earthing network at the fault bus
    i_earth, earth_buses — \(I_E\) and the group of buses whose electrode currents it sums
    i_fault — \(3I_0\), the scaled source currents that reach the fault

    The EPR ratio¶

    The reduction factor \(r\) at the fault bus is defined as

    \[ r(f) = \frac{|u_{\text{fault}}^{\text{(with mutual)}}(f)|} {|u_{\text{fault}}^{\text{(without mutual)}}(f)|}. \]

    groundinsight obtains the denominator by re-solving the same network with all mutual-coupling Norton sources removed. For a single shielded line directly between source and fault with identical impedances the expression collapses to the familiar analytical form

    \[ r = \left| 1 - \frac{Z_{\text{mutual}}}{Z_{\text{self}}} \right|. \]

    The same closed form applies to a fully symmetric ring with the fault diametrically opposite the source — the Norton injections in the two ring halves are then perfectly anti-parallel and superpose to the same expression as the single-line case. More generally it holds in any network whose branches all have the same ratio \(k = Z_m/Z_s\): the Norton injections then telescope along every route to \((1-k)\) times the source injection, and every potential with coupling is \((1-k)\) times the one without.

    Reduction factor on a current basis¶

    The ratio above is structurally blind to the impedance at the fault bus. Both solves use the same Y, so changing the diagonal entry Y_ff is a rank-1 update whose effect cancels in the quotient. On the six-station feeder of the decomposition example, sweeping the electrode of the faulted station from 0.01 Ω to 100 Ω leaves value at 0.310003, while \(Z_E\) and the potential rise both grow by a factor of 80 (0.0099 → 0.796 Ω, 8.3 → 668 V) and value_current falls from 0.278 to 0.168. That is not a numerical artefact — the closed form in Characterising a location derives it.

    ResultReductionFactor.value_current is the measured definition alongside it: the total earth-return current over the total fault current,

    \[ r_I(f) = \frac{|\underline{I}_E(f)|}{|\underline{I}_F(f)|}. \]

    I_E is the sum of the electrode currents of every bus that feeds the soil — from the fault outwards in every direction, a ring or a mesh having more than one, up to where the potential profile turns — not the electrode current at the faulted station. In a cable network with continuous shields the stations are bonded to one another, so the current spreads along the shields and leaks into the soil at every one of them; the earthing current is distributed over the whole bonded system by construction. Measured on a six-station feeder, counting only the faulted station understates r_I by a factor of 1.8 to 4.7 depending on where the fault sits.

    Two things make the sum less obvious than it looks.

    Summing over every bus gives exactly zero. Whatever enters the soil somewhere leaves it somewhere else, so Kirchhoff at the whole network forces Σ I_a = 0. A group has to be selected — and "every bus except the faulted one" is not that group: by the same law it collapses back to the electrode current of the faulted station alone.

    The group cannot be found from the potential profile. The crossing between the two groups generally falls between two stations rather than on one, and when the fault sits close to the infeed the profile may never come near zero at all — it passes through a shallow minimum and rises again. On the verification feeder with the fault one station from the infeed, |EPR| runs 91.4, 19.6, 18.7, 18.1, 17.7, 17.5 V: there is no crossing to find.

    What is unambiguous per bus is the direction of its electrode current, so the split is made in the complex plane: the group is the set of phasors in one half-plane, and the half-plane is the one whose sum has the largest magnitude. That needs no angle nominated and no tolerance tuned, and it reduces to the obvious answer whenever the two groups are cleanly opposed. Both groups carry the same sum with opposite signs, so |I_E| does not depend on which is called which; the fault bus anchors the naming.

    ResultReductionFactor reports i_earth (the ampere value) and earth_buses (the group) next to the ratio, because the split is a modelling statement and should be inspectable. The separation of the two groups says how cleanly the phasors separated; below 0.9 the electrode currents are spread in angle and a single scalar earth-return current is a coarser description than it looks, and run_fault says so (ModelAssumptionWarning, code EARTH_GROUP_SPREAD). Where no electrode carries a noticeable share of the fault current — the fault current returns entirely through shields and conductors — there is no group, and \(I_E\) and value_current are None (EARTH_GROUP_UNDEFINED). res_all_impedances() carries both factors.

    The denominator is every source that feeds the fault. i_fault is the sum of the scaled currents of all sources that reach the fault, a source on the fault bus itself included. Such a source injects and withdraws its current at the same bus; it adds to \(3I_0\) but not to \(I_E\). With 1000 A from a remote infeed and 500 A from a source on the fault bus, value_current is exactly two thirds of its value without the local source, while i_earth and value do not change. The reduction factor of a line in the sense of the standard counts the remote infeed only; divide i_earth by it where a local source is present.

    Which one is the EN 50522 reduction factor¶

    value_current, r = |I_E|/|3I_0|. The norm writes the earthing voltage as

    \[ U_E = 3 I_0 \cdot Z_E \cdot r, \]

    with two quantities that groundinsight reports at the fault bus (ResultReductionFactor):

    • u_earthing is U_E, the EPR at the fault bus — the same complex number as the fault bus's uepr_freq;
    • z_earthing is Z_E, the driving-point impedance of the earthing network at the fault bus: one ampere injected there against remote earth, every electrode and shield in place, no inductive coupling, no Thevenin loop closure. It is what an earthing-impedance measurement at the station sees, the stations bonded through cable shields included, and it is the reported grounding impedance.

    The chain is a physical statement about these, not an identity: it holds where the norm's assumptions hold. On a long homogeneous cable run with the fault far from the infeed it closes with r = |1 - Z_m/Z_s| (the factor value) on the ladder limit Z_E = -Z'/2 + sqrt(Z'^2/4 + Z_e Z') to better than 1e-5 (tests/test_audit_pass19_physics.py, 64 sections). Away from that case — a short feeder, the infeed close by, large electrodes — |U_E| and r \cdot 3I_0 \cdot |Z_E| differ, and the difference measures how far the installation is from the norm's lumped picture; nothing raises or warns.

    The long run, built and compared with the closed form (reduction_factors.py; 64 sections of 0.5 km, stations 2 Ω, infeed 0.2 Ω, 1 kA, illustrative per-km values):

    N_SECTIONS = 64
SECTION_KM = 0.5
Z_STATION = 2.0          # Ohm, electrode of every station
I_FAULT = 1000.0         # A RMS, 3I_0 at 50 Hz

cable = gi.BranchType(
    name="cable", grounding_conductor=True,
    self_impedance_formula="(0.29 + 0.625*j*f/50)*l",     # Z_s' (illustrative)
    mutual_impedance_formula="(0.049 + 0.625*j*f/50)*l",  # Z_m' (illustrative)
)
station = gi.BusType(name="station", system_type="Station", voltage_level=20.0,
                     impedance_formula=repr(Z_STATION))
infeed = gi.BusType(name="infeed", system_type="Substation", voltage_level=110.0,
                    impedance_formula="0.2")

net = gi.create_network(name="long_run", frequencies=[50.0])
gi.create_bus(name="N0", type=infeed, network=net)
for i in range(1, N_SECTIONS + 1):
    gi.create_bus(name=f"N{i}", type=station, network=net)
for i in range(N_SECTIONS):
    gi.create_branch(name=f"L{i + 1}", type=cable, from_bus=f"N{i}",
                     to_bus=f"N{i + 1}", length=SECTION_KM, network=net)
gi.create_source(name="infeed", bus="N0", values={50.0: I_FAULT}, network=net)
gi.create_fault(name="F", bus=f"N{N_SECTIONS}", network=net)

rf = gi.run_fault(net, "F").reduction_factor
u_e = complex(rf.u_earthing[50.0])        # U_E: EPR of the fault bus
z_e = complex(rf.z_earthing[50.0])        # Z_E: driving-point impedance there
r_epr = rf.value[50.0]                    # EPR ratio, |1 - Z_m/Z_s| here
r_current = rf.value_current[50.0]        # |I_E| / |3I_0|

    z_series = (0.29 + 0.625j) * SECTION_KM   # Z' of one section
z_mutual = (0.049 + 0.625j) * SECTION_KM
k = z_mutual / z_series
z_inf = -z_series / 2 + cmath.sqrt(z_series ** 2 / 4 + Z_STATION * z_series)
u_closed = abs(1 - k) * I_FAULT * abs(z_inf)
    Quantity Model Closed form
    value 0.349781 \(\lvert 1 - k\rvert\) = 0.349781
    value_current 0.359015 —
    \(Z_E\) 0.6249 + 0.3081j Ω \(Z_\infty\) = 0.6249 + 0.3081j Ω
    \(\lvert U_E\rvert\) 243.708 V \(\lvert 1-k\rvert \cdot I \cdot \lvert Z_\infty\rvert\) = 243.708 V
    value · 3I0 · |ZE| 243.708 V
    value_current · 3I0 · |ZE| 250.142 V

    The chain closes with the coupling factor of the cable. value_current lies 2.6 % above it here: along the run the electrode currents decay and rotate with the complex propagation constant of the ladder, the half-plane split leaves out those that point backwards, and the sum of the rest exceeds \(\lvert 1-k\rvert \cdot I\).

    The current-weighted mean potential of the earth group and that mean divided by I_E — which release 0.6.0 reported as u_earthing / z_earthing — are kept as u_earthing_group / z_earthing_group. On that pair the chain closes to machine precision, but only because z_earthing_group is defined as u_earthing_group / I_E; it checks nothing. On the long run above they are 138.83 V and 0.3867 Ω, 43 % and 44.5 % below the fault-bus EPR and the driving-point impedance, which is why they are no longer reported as U_E and Z_E: taken into a touch-voltage assessment they are unconservative.

    The fault current the factor divides by is stored as i_fault (sum(scaling * I_src) over the sources that reach the fault).

    value uses a different reference: the mutual coupling removed, but the cable shield still in place as a metallic return path. Most of the current still comes back through the shield rather than through the soil, so that reference voltage is far below Z_E \cdot 3I_0 (the norm's r = 1, the whole fault current through the earthing impedance). Both factors are correct answers; they answer different questions.

    The two factors are not two computations of one number¶

    They coincide in exactly one case, and it is worth knowing which. For a route with shield impedance \(Z_s\), mutual impedance \(Z_m\) and station electrodes summing to \(Z_E\) along the earth-return path,

    \[ r_\text{coupling} = \frac{Z_s - Z_m}{Z_s}, \qquad r_I = \frac{Z_s - Z_m}{Z_s + Z_E}, \]

    so their ratio is the current divider \(Z_s/(Z_s + Z_E)\) and nothing else. The first is the ideally bonded limit — the tabulated property of the cable, \(1 - Z_m/Z_s\), independent of the station earths by construction. The second is what the earthing system of this network actually passes into the soil.

    Verified against the closed form to machine precision over five decades of electrode impedance on a two-section line with \(Z_s = 0.2 + 0.4\mathrm{j}\ \Omega\):

    electrode per station \(r_\text{coupling}\) \(r_I\) ratio \(Z_s/(Z_s+Z_E)\)
    10 Ω 0.500000 0.011067 0.022135 0.022135
    1 Ω 0.500000 0.100000 0.200000 0.200000
    0.1 Ω 0.500000 0.395285 0.790569 0.790569
    0.01 Ω 0.500000 0.489820 0.979639 0.979639
    10 µΩ 0.500000 0.499990 0.999980 0.999980

    A wide gap is therefore a statement about the network, not a defect: the stations are not bonded well enough for the cable's tabulated reduction factor to describe what the soil sees. groundinsight logs the divider at INFO where it falls below 0.5, rather than as a warning — with ordinary station electrodes it is the normal case, and a log that warns about the normal case stops being read.

    Frequency dependence of the reduction factor¶

    For a shielded cable the impedances split into a resistive and an inductive part,

    \[ Z_{\text{self}}(f) = R + j\,\omega L, \qquad Z_{\text{mutual}}(f) = j\,\omega M, \qquad \omega = 2\pi f. \]

    With full coupling between the faulted phase and the grounding conductor (the geometric ideal \(M = L\)) the closed form simplifies to

    \[ r(f) \;=\; \left| 1 - \frac{j\,\omega L}{R + j\,\omega L} \right| \;=\; \frac{R}{\sqrt{R^{2} + (\omega L)^{2}}}. \]

    Two limits follow directly:

    • \(f \to 0\): \(\omega L \to 0\), so \(Z_{\text{mutual}}/Z_{\text{self}} \to 0\) and \(r \to 1\). At DC the shield carries no induced current, the entire fault current flows through the local earth path and the reduction factor equals 1.
    • \(f \to \infty\): the imaginary parts dominate, so \(Z_{\text{mutual}}/Z_{\text{self}} \to M/L = 1\) and \(r \to 0\). At high frequency the shield short-circuits the inductive coupling, almost the entire fault current returns metallically and the EPR collapses.

    In a real cable the resistive part is small compared with \(\omega L\) already at power frequency, which is why MV cables typically reach \(r \approx 0.3 \ldots 0.4\) at 50 Hz and the reduction factor decays quickly above one or two hundred Hz. This convergence is exercised explicitly by tests/test_topology_and_reduction.py::test_reduction_factor_sweep_*, which sweeps a single MV cable section and a 20-bus symmetric ring from 50 Hz to 5 kHz and asserts both the closed form above and the monotonic decay towards zero.

    Grounding impedance¶

    ResultGroundingImpedance.value is Z_E in the EN 50522 sense: the driving-point impedance of the earthing network at the fault bus, identical to ResultReductionFactor.z_earthing,

    \[ Z_G(f) = Z_E(f) = Z_\text{dp}(F, f) = \left(Y_E(f)^{-1}\right)_{FF}, \]

    with \(Y_E\) the nodal matrix of electrodes and shields alone — without the inductive coupling and without the Thevenin loop closures of voltage sources, which belong to the fault loop rather than to the earthing system. It is the impedance an earthing-impedance measurement at the station sees, the stations bonded to it through cable shields included; gi.bus_response(...) (Möbius form) and gi.analyze_cuts(...) (without voltage sources) reproduce it. Note that it is not the station electrodes in parallel either — the shield sections between them add to it.

    Z_E is None (with a ModelAssumptionWarning EARTHING_IMPEDANCE_UNDEFINED) where the fault side reaches remote earth only through the loop closure of a voltage source: no bus connected to the fault bus through shields carries an electrode. That is decided from the structure of the earthing network, not from the solver — the matrix of such a group of buses is singular only up to rounding and a solve returns a meaningless 1e15 Ω. In bus_response, where the electrode at the studied bus is the fault side's only one, every ampere leaves through it: Z_E = Z_B + BusResponse.z_fault_ideal.

    The quotient of the earth group's mean potential by I_E, which release 0.6.0 reported as the grounding impedance, is kept as z_earthing_group; on a long homogeneous run it is 44.5 % below Z_E. The definition before 0.6.0, \(u_{\text{EPR}}/(r \cdot I_{\text{fault}})\), mixed the coupling ratio into an impedance.

    It is exposed per frequency through net.res_all_impedances(), which has one row per (fault, frequency) and no RMS row: a grounding impedance is defined for a frequency, and there is no meaningful way to reduce a set of complex impedances at different frequencies to one scalar. The RMS rows live on net.res_buses() and net.res_branches(), where they aggregate quantities (\(\sqrt{\sum_f |x(f)|^2}\)), and are marked there by the is_rms column.

    Results stored with the earlier definition¶

    A result saved by a release that reported the group quantities as \(U_E\) and \(Z_E\) — in a JSON project file or a database — still loads. Its stored values are moved to u_earthing_group / z_earthing_group, u_earthing, z_earthing and the grounding impedance stay empty, and the result carries an info diagnostic, code LEGACY_EARTHING_DEFINITION, that says why. Run the fault again with run_fault to obtain \(U_E\) and \(Z_E\) in the current sense; nothing is recomputed silently on load.
